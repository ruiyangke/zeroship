# Writing for Gather customers

Write for someone choosing dinner, checking a delivery, or asking for help.
Every heading, label, hint, action, empty state and error should help them take
the next step or understand what happens to their order.

- Use familiar shopping language: meals, delivery address, order total and
  delivery deadline. Keep database, quote, workflow, reservation and simulation
  terminology out of the customer journey.
- Make transactional headings and buttons concrete. Use “Review your order,”
  “Save address” and “Cancel this box.” Keep brand language on discovery pages;
  a missing address or failed payment needs a clear explanation and action.
- Show information where it helps. Put coverage behind “View delivery areas.”
  Ask for the address fields used in the selected country. A field that is not
  needed should not appear alongside an explanation of why it is not needed.
- Distinguish a weekly plan from an existing order. Say whether an action
  affects the current box or future deliveries. State payment amounts and any
  deadlines before asking for confirmation.
- Keep copy faithful to actual behavior. A payment awaiting verification is not
  a confirmed order. A deletion request awaiting review is not a deleted
  account. Do not invent delivery, refund or notification promises.
- Identify the preview through the shared store notice. Put payment scenario
  controls in the staff workspace. Avoid repeating developer explanations in
  address hints, checkout headings, buttons, account messages and FAQs.
- Translate the customer's intent into natural language. Review Chinese
  independently from English. Use everyday address and shopping terms, and keep
  interface and bundled sample translations in Lingui catalogs. Staff-authored
  recipe translations belong to the approved recipe version.
- Translate complete messages. Do not assemble consent or error sentences from
  fragments. Keep names, addresses and customer-written messages intact.
- Errors should name what the customer can do next. Never show serialized
  validation output, operation keys or database details in a form.

Verify copy in the rendered journey, including narrow screens, focus order,
screen-reader labels and unsuccessful submissions. Catalog compilation checks
translation completeness; browser checks establish that labels match the
controls and actions customers actually use.
